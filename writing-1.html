Writing 01

The Self Attached to Things

Russell Belk’s concept of the extended self explores how external possessions and objects can become incorporated into our sense of identity and function as forms of external scaffolding for the self.

In moments of crisis, the self is no longer an abstract concept. It becomes attached to the body, objects, environment, and social roles through which identity is ordinarily maintained.

Sandro Giordano’s photographic series In Extremis can be approached through this psychological lens. Following a cycling accident, Giordano described an impulse to reconstruct the experience of falling and to externalize inner falls through photography.

From a psychological perspective, the objects present in these images can be considered forms of external identity scaffolding: structures through which people maintain a relatively stable image of themselves within the social world.

At the critical moment, these structures collapse. What remains is the body without the usual effort to maintain appearance, control, or social presentation.

The reconstruction of falling can therefore also be understood as an attempt to transform a passive experience into an active creative act. The images become mirrors through which we can consider the often invisible supports on which our own psychological balance depends.

The therapeutic function Giordano describes should, however, be understood as his personal account rather than evidence that art directly replaces psychotherapy. Art may instead serve as a complementary way of examining experience, identity, and defensive processes.
