Art and Psychology

This space explores the relationship between visual experience and psychological meaning, with attention to images, perception, identity, and the ways visual forms can become a medium for understanding human experience.

Visual Experience

Images are not only objects of observation. They can participate in perception, memory, attention, imagination, and the formation of personal meaning. This section considers how visual experience becomes psychologically significant in everyday life.

Image in Psychological Practice

Drawing, photography, and visual materials can sometimes provide alternative ways of communicating experience, especially when verbal expression is limited. Their use in psychological practice requires contextual interpretation and should not be treated as a standalone method of diagnosis.
